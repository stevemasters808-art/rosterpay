How penalty rates work in these awards

General Retail Industry Award (MA000004)

Ordinary hours can be rostered 7am to 9pm Monday to Friday, 7am to 6pm Saturday and 9am to 6pm Sunday. Full-time and part-time staff get 125% after 6pm on weekdays and on Saturdays, 150% on Sundays and 225% on public holidays. Casuals get 25% more on top at every step: 125% for ordinary hours, 150% evenings and Saturdays, 175% Sundays, 250% public holidays. Work past 9 ordinary hours in a day, past 38 in a week, or outside the span is overtime: 150% for the first 3 hours Monday to Saturday then 200%, 200% all day Sunday, 250% on a public holiday (casuals add 25% to each). Level 1 adult minimum is $27.81 an hour; level 8 is $33.99.

Juniors at levels 1 to 3 are paid a percentage of the adult rate: 45% under 16, then 50%, 60%, 70%, 80% at 19, and 90% at 20 (100% after 6 months). From 1 December 2026, 18 and 19 year olds with more than 6 months' service step up 5% every 1 December and 1 July until they reach the adult rate.

Hospitality Industry (General) Award (MA000009)

Monday to Friday 7am to 7pm is the ordinary rate. From 7pm to midnight add $2.95 for each hour or part hour; from midnight to 7am add $4.42. Saturdays pay 125%, Sundays 150%, public holidays 225%; casuals get 150%, 175% and 250% including their loading. Overtime applies after 11.5 ordinary hours in a shift (12 for casuals) or 38 in a week: 150% for the first 2 hours Monday to Friday, then 200%, and 200% all weekend. Hospitality casuals are not paid the casual loading on overtime. Only the single highest penalty applies to any hour. Level 1 adult minimum is $26.44 an hour; introductory is $25.74. Juniors get 50% under 17, 60% at 17, 70% at 18, 85% at 19 and the full rate at 20; juniors who serve alcohol are always paid the adult rate.

Public holidays in New South Wales

NSW has 13 public holidays a year. Labour Day is the first Monday in October. Australia Day, Anzac Day and Christmas are moved or doubled when they fall on a weekend, so a shift on the Monday after can attract public holiday rates. The August Bank Holiday is not a public holiday for retail or hospitality workers.

What this calculator does not do

It does not cover enterprise agreements, other awards (Fast Food, Restaurant, Pharmacy, Hair and Beauty, Clerks), allowances (laundry, meal, first aid, cold work), shiftwork rosters, apprentices, annualised salaries, leave, superannuation or tax. Minimum engagement rules (3 hours in Retail, 2 hours for Hospitality casuals) are not applied. It is a first check, not a ruling: the award and the Fair Work Ombudsman Pay and Conditions Tool prevail.

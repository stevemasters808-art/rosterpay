# Ad Revenue Won't Carry These Five Apps

**Bottom line:** a solo Australian developer with an Android phone, a Windows/Linux PC and AI-generated code can cheaply build all five finalists, but none of them is validated as a business that pays worthwhile money through advertising, and the evidence points the other way. The ad model built from the only public eCPM benchmarks (Appodeal, Oct–Dec 2024, games-framed) and the idea-specific usage patterns says the AUD 100 AdMob payout threshold needs roughly **2,000 to 20,000 monthly active users** depending on the app, and AUD 1,000 a month needs **20,000 to 180,000**, while the best comparable solo-built Australian apps in these niches have reached 100 to 5,000 lifetime installs in two to nine years (loadmate ≤4,999 installs in 40 months; FishOz 100+ in two years; Record My Hours 10K+ since 2017). The deep validation also overturned several premises from the broad scan: the caravan "40% overloaded" statistic is recycled 2023 police data, an all-states offline fish-limits app already exists (iDfish, since 2016), the sound meter's three "differentiators" already ship free or near-free on Android, and the "no-sync" expense splitter is a strict subset of what Splid offers at 4.9 stars. The honest reading is "none sufficiently validated yet": the Australian award penalty-rate checker is the only finalist with an uncontested Play keyword and a near-100% tier-1 audience, and even it is a once-per-payslip tool whose ad economics only work if it also becomes the user's shift log. Confidence in the direction of this verdict is high; confidence in the exact magnitudes is moderate, because no measured session data exists for any of these apps, no utility-specific eCPM benchmark was found, and several demand signals (Google Trends, Reddit, AppBrain app pages, most state fisheries and Fair Work pages) were unreachable.

## The verdict: not validated as ad businesses, with high confidence on direction

The question has three parts, and each gets a clear answer. Can this developer build small, useful apps with AI alone? Yes: a maths teacher with a hobbyist background shipped a native Kotlin/Jetpack Compose app to Google Play in about three weeks using Claude ([HN, 31 May 2026](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48344627)), a vibe-coded iOS workout app was approved after a month ([HN, 20 Feb 2026](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47091822)), and every one of the five finalists is pure client-side code with no backend. Can those apps make worthwhile money primarily through ads? Not on the evidence gathered: the per-user ad yield of a short-session utility is a few tenths of a US cent per active day, and none of the five has a plausible route to the audience size that would turn that into AUD 500 or 1,000 a month. Is iOS practical without Apple hardware? Technically yes, through cloud builds that cost nothing at hobby volume, but it adds a fixed US$99 a year (≈ AUD 141), a documented Guideline 4.2 rejection risk for minimal utilities, and the unsolved problem of never touching a real iPhone, so it is a cost centre until an Android version has already proven demand.

Three facts drive the verdict. First, foreground time is scarce: four of the five finalists are consulted for one to three minutes, between once a week and once a year, and AdMob forbids ads in the background, on launch, mid-task or after every action ([AdMob interstitial guidance](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6201362)), so each active user yields two or three banner impressions and at most one full-screen ad per session. Second, audience ceilings are low and observable: the only incumbent with traction in the caravan niche sits at the 1K+ Play tier after 40 months ([loadmate on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.loadmate&hl=en_AU)); a modern WA-only fishing-rules app has 100+ Android installs after two years ([FishOz on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.michaf.ozfish&hl=en_AU)); an iOS penalty-rate app functionally identical to the finalist has one rating four weeks after launch ([PayJar on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/payjar-shift-pay-calculator/id6813980852)). Third, where the audience is global (sound meter, splitter), the eCPM collapses to the blended global average, roughly US$1.65 per thousand interstitials and US$0.13 per thousand banners on Android ([Appodeal eCPM report, Q4 2024](https://appodeal.com/the-mobile-ecpm-report-updated-q4-2024-view)), and the incumbents add 100K to 380K installs a month each ([AppGoblin on Splend](https://www.appgoblin.info/apps/com.splendapps.decibel); [AppGoblin on Splitwise](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.Splitwise.SplitwiseMobile)).

**Confidence: high that ads alone will not reach AUD 500/month for any finalist within a year; moderate on the exact user counts** (inputs are modelled, not measured, and the eCPM dataset is for games); **low-to-moderate on the relative ranking of the two weakest-evidenced ideas** (sound meter and fish limits), because their demand signals were the ones most affected by blocked sources. The report applies one exchange rate throughout: the RBA rate of **1 AUD = 0.7010 USD on 21 October 2026**, so 1 USD ≈ AUD 1.4265 ([RBA exchange rates](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/frequency/exchange-rates.html)). The tooling notes originally converted at the ECB rate of 1.4217 for the same day; those figures are restated here at the RBA rate, which moves them by under 0.4%.

## What the 1-3-5 scores mean before any score is shown

Both scales are defined for this specific developer: AI-generated code, Android-first on Windows/Linux, no Mac or iPhone, no backend, and a stated wish for minimal development and upkeep. They are judgements applied to evidence, not measurements, and the finalist validations show that scan-stage scores tend to fall by one to two points once incumbents and usage are checked properly.

**Ease of building.** A **5** is pure offline arithmetic or UI with no runtime permissions, no native libraries beyond the AdMob SDK, and static data that rarely changes; it is a weekend build whose only forced maintenance is Play's annual target-API bump and policy forms. A **3** needs one sensor or runtime permission (microphone, camera, location), a local database with notifications, or a bundled dataset that must be refreshed several times a year; it is a few weeks' work with recurring data releases or exposure to OS-API churn. A **1** needs background services, sync or a backend, licensed or safety-critical data, platform-fragile APIs such as Android clipboard access, or iOS-only behaviour that cannot be tested without Apple hardware.

**Commercial potential (ad-funded).** A **5** has evidenced, large, recurring demand in tier-1 eCPM markets (Australia, US, UK, Canada), weak or degraded incumbents, multi-minute foreground sessions several times a week, winnable store keywords, and a plausible path to AUD 1,000 a month from ads alone. A **3** has evidenced demand and a weak incumbent on at least one platform but one structural drag (low frequency, short sessions, small Australia-only volume, or a free official alternative), so AUD 100 to 500 a month is plausible only with a one-off "remove ads" purchase or a web companion page alongside the ads. A **1** has unproven demand or 1M+ well-rated free incumbents, is absorbed by the operating system, runs in sessions of seconds, or sits mostly outside tier-1 geographies, so ads are unlikely to clear the AUD 100 payout threshold in a typical month. Scores of 2 and 4 fall between these anchors; half-points mark cases where the evidence genuinely straddles two anchors.

### Penalty-rate checker: real pain, free data, weak sessions

**Target user and frequency.** The Fair Work Commission's 2025 wage review put award-reliant employees at 20.7% of all employees, about 2.61 million people, and the 2026 decision reportedly at 21.1% or almost 2.8 million (the 2025 figure is quoted via an economist's blog because the FWC PDFs returned 403; the 2026 figure is search-snippet only) ([Bill Mitchell quoting 2025 FWCFB 3500](https://billmitchell.org/blog/?p=62604); [FairWork Mate case summary](https://fairworkmate.com.au/cases/2025-fwcfb-3500)). The Fair Work Ombudsman recovered **$358 million for more than 249,000 underpaid workers in 2024-25** (fairwork.gov.au blocked direct fetch; confirmed via trade press) ([HCA Mag](https://www.hcamag.com/au/specialisation/employment-law/fwo-recovers-358-million-for-over-249000-underpaid-workers/554646)). The audience is almost entirely Australian, young, casual or part-time and lower-income. No usage data exists for any pay-checker app; the structure of the task (compare a payslip once per weekly or fortnightly cycle) implies 0.5 to 1 deliberate session a week of one to three minutes, rising to three to six short sessions a week only if the app is also the shift log, which is what the one dated PayJar reviewer describes ("calculating my pay every week... also works as a great calendar for my shifts", 8 Oct 2026) ([PayJar on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/payjar-shift-pay-calculator/id6813980852)).

**Alternatives and the reason to switch.** On Android AU, the government's Record My Hours app (10K+ installs, 2.9 stars, 114 reviews, ~38% one-star, updated April 2026) records hours but does not calculate pay; a 2021 reviewer wrote that the ability to "add your hourly, so the app calculates all the penalty rates... would have me use this app", and 2024–2026 reviews describe lost records ([Record My Hours on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=au.gov.fairwork.recordmyhours&hl=en_AU)). The only award-aware Android app is MyRoster (1K+, 3.6 stars from 18 reviews, subscription required, built by a nurse for health-sector awards); it advertises "every penalty, allowance and overtime hour", while a 13 Sep 2026 reviewer confirms "the OT pay calculation is incorrect" and a July reviewer was locked out after paying ([MyRoster on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.jamielollback.myroster&hl=en_AU)). A Play search for "penalty rates calculator" returns 30 apps, none award-aware ([Play search](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=penalty+rates+calculator&c=apps&hl=en_AU)). On iOS AU, MyRoster is 4.84 stars from 49 ratings at $6.99 a month ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/myroster-for-shift-workers/id6745118804)), and PayJar (released 24 Sep 2026) implements the finalist's exact scope for Retail and Hospitality awards, including payslip comparison, midnight splits and "highest rate rather than stacking", with no ads, no account and a $14.99 one-off Pro tier; it has one rating ([PayJar](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/payjar-shift-pay-calculator/id6813980852)). Six further 2026 iOS entrants have 0–7 ratings each. The "ShiftTrak" app named in the earlier scan could not be found on either store and is treated as unverified. On the web, the FWO's PACT tool offers Pay and Shift calculators but is session-based and online ([PACT](https://calculate.fairwork.gov.au/findyouraward)). The reason to switch is therefore "free, on Android, offline, no account, remembers my shifts", not a calculation nobody else does.

**Evidence of unmet need, smallest version, maintenance.** The need is confirmed by the 2021 Record My Hours request, the 2.6M workforce and the uncontested Play keyword; the demand ceiling is bounded by Ubersuggest's **2,400 Australian searches a month for "penalty rates calculator"** (SEO difficulty 15, CPC US$7.79, data dated 19 Apr 2026), only 30 for "shift pay calculator", and 22,200 for "pay calculator australia", which is tax intent and a different product (data dated 6 Sep 2026). No US or UK volumes were pulled; autocomplete shows US "overtime calculator" demand is state-law driven and UK demand clusters around NHS bands. The smallest useful version is a two-award (General Retail and Hospitality) offline calculator with shift entry, employment type, classification and age band, a per-shift breakdown and a "compare with payslip gross" field; PayJar proves one person can ship it. Data comes from the FWC Modern Awards Pay Database, free and CC BY 4.0, whose terms disclaim accuracy, make the award prevail, forbid implying FWC endorsement and impose an indemnity on anyone who re-supplies the data ([FWC developer terms of use](https://developer.fwc.gov.au/terms-of-use); [important information](https://devdeveloper.fwc.gov.au/important-information)). Rates rose 3.5% from 1 July 2025 and 4.75% from 1 July 2026 ([APSC Circular 2026/02](https://www.apsc.gov.au/resources/circulars-guidance-and-advice/circular-202602-annual-wage-review-updating-australian-government-awards)), and junior rates in Retail, Fast Food and Pharmacy begin phasing out on 1 December 2026 (search-snippet only) ([Employment Hero](https://employmenthero.com/blog/junior-pay-rates-changes/)), so budget two to four data releases a year. Play's Deceptive Behavior policy bars implying government affiliation, so the title and icon must avoid "Fair Work" styling ([Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888077)). Build difficulty is low but the logic must be exactly right: penalty windows, midnight splits, state public holidays and casual-loading interactions make a wrong app worse than none.

**Acquisition and monetisation fit.** The cheapest channels are an exact-match Play title, a static SEO page per state that embeds the same calculator, content timed to 1 July and the 1 December junior-rate change, and short videos aimed at casual retail and hospitality workers; MyRoster's traction came from a nurse community and near-daily releases, PayJar's silent launch produced one rating. Competitors monetise with a one-off purchase (PayJar) or a subscription (MyRoster), and the user base is low-income; ads are defensible only as the acquisition wedge ("the free one on Android") with an optional A$5–10 one-off "remove ads and export" purchase. The $5.48 AU CPC on the head term suggests a web companion page may out-earn in-app ads at this volume. Validated scores: **ease 4, commercial 2 for ads alone and 3 with IAP and a web page; status partially validated**.

### Caravan weight calculator: a weekend build without a business

**Target user and frequency.** Australia had roughly **937,000 registered RVs in 2025, more than 817,000 of them towables**, with about 48,000 new RVs entering the register in the year to June 2026 ([CIAA State of the Industry 2026](https://www.caravanindustry.com.au/state-of-the-industry-2026-resilience-in-a-changing-market/); [CIAA counters](https://www.caravanindustry.com.au/who-we-are/)). Every dated frequency signal points to episodic use: the only owner voice on the Android incumbent is a 22 July 2026 one-star review, "$30 p/yr really for an app I'd only use once a year" ([loadmate on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.loadmate&hl=en_AU)); the US app TowCheck added trip reminders in October 2026 specifically to drive re-opens ([TowCheck on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/towcheck-towing-calculator/id6793156138)). The compliance pressure is real but the headline statistic is not new: the June 2026 Ford article that reports "almost 40 per cent" of caravans over ATM at a NSW roadside station footnotes a 44-rig dataset from late-2023 mobile weighing operations, so the earlier scan's "NSW weigh-station report, June 2026" is a 2023 police sample recycled in manufacturer content ([Ford From the Road, 23 Jun 2026](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/img/en/articles/2026/caravans-outgrown-pickups-to-tow-them)); the other enforcement figures are Victoria 2017 (41 of 71 overweight) and Queensland 2023 ([NRMA](https://www.mynrma.com.au/open-road/advice-and-how-to/caravanning-and-camping/overweight-caravans); [Grey Nomads, Nov 2023](https://www.thegreynomads.com.au/?p=63883)).

**Alternatives and the reason to switch.** Android AU is thin but not empty: loadmate (4Thought RV; 1K+ installs, 3.5 stars from 14 ratings, no ads, Pro A$29.99 a year, first listed June 2023) advertises RigScore, axle loads and "no account needed"; its 5-star review confirms it "helped me realise the difference between GTM and ATM" while its 1-star review confirms once-a-year use ([loadmate.io](https://loadmate.io/)). Weigh That Tow (1K+, ads plus A$3.99–17.99 purchases, no displayed rating) already bundles tyre pressures, fuel-tank modelling and trip profiles ([Weigh That Tow on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dknss.WeighThatTow&hl=en_AU)); Ready2Tow (10+ installs) gates full figures behind "Tow Pro" ([Ready2Tow on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ready2roam.tow&hl=en_AU)); Caravan Mate (A$4.99) has 100+ installs after about five years ([Caravan Mate on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.gmail.towball.caravanmatetowing&hl=en_AU)). On iOS AU, loadmate is 5.0 stars from 7 ratings and the paid Caravan Towing Weight (A$4.99) is 1 star from 1 rating ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/caravan-towing-weight/id1580163802)); in the US, RV Tow Check (US$3.99) sits at 2.03 stars from 36 ratings, unmaintained since 2021, while TowCheck and RigIQ launched free in August 2026 ([RV Tow Check](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rv-tow-check/id1232539021); [RigIQ](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rigiq-rv-weight-safety/id6795268263)). Free web calculators (SafeTow, Ford's towing calculator), club spreadsheets, $150–250 mobile weighing services and free council weigh days complete the picture. The only open position is "simplest free offline one-screen calculator", and the app is a companion to weighing rather than a substitute, because tow-ball mass cannot be calculated without a scale.

**Smallest version, build, acquisition and monetisation.** The MVP is plate inputs for vehicle and van, a payload list, traffic-light margin bars for ATM, GTM, GVM, GCM, tow-ball and towbar limits, an offline glossary with a worked example, and a shareable summary; it is a few hundred lines of arithmetic with no permissions, and every incumbent already ships saved rigs, so they are table stakes rather than a retention lever. Transport for NSW tells owners to weigh and offers no calculator ([TfNSW caravans](https://www.transport.nsw.gov.au/roadsafety/road-users/drivers/caravans)); every incumbent frames output as an estimate that does not replace a weighbridge, which is the liability pattern to copy. Australian search demand is small: **"caravan weight calculator" 480 searches a month (SEO difficulty 8)**, "towing capacity calculator" 260, a calculator-intent cluster of roughly 1,300–2,000 a month, with "tow ball weight scales" at 590 showing buyers think of it as a measurement problem (Ubersuggest, locId 2036, data dated Nov 2025–Sep 2026). US demand was not measured; US RV shipments were 342,220 in 2025 ([RVIA](https://www.rvia.org/reports-trends/rv-shipment-reports/2025-12/rv-shipments-end-2025-342220-units-modest-25-growth-over-2024)) but use different terminology (GVWR, CCC, SAE J2807). Acquisition channels exist and are concentrated (two Facebook groups above 200,000 members in 2018 counts, Grey Nomads and GoRV press, council weigh days) ([Caravanning With Kids, 2018](https://caravanningwithkids.com.au/?p=8762)), yet the best-funded incumbent reached only the 1K+ tier in 40 months. Subscriptions and paid-upfront have both been punished in reviews; free with a light banner, one capped interstitial and an A$3–5 one-off "Pro" fits the once-a-year perception. Validated scores: **ease 4.5, commercial 1.5; not validated as an ad-funded business**, validated only as a cheap build with a defined test.

### Fish size and bag limits: the pain is real, the "no competitor" premise is false

**Target user and frequency.** About **4.2 million adult Australians fish each year** across 28 million fishing days, but two-thirds fish fewer than five days a year and only about 15% fish ten or more days (FRDC National Recreational Fishing Survey, 2018–21, published 2023; FRDC pages were behind a Cloudflare challenge so these figures are search-snippet only) ([FRDC NRFS](https://www.frdc.com.au/about-recreational-fishing/nrfs)). The realistic active base is the roughly 630,000 avid fishers, consulting rules one to three times per fishing day for about a minute, often with no signal: QLD app reviewers in March and May 2026 complain that the official app "needs to connect to the internet for an update and is unusable" in remote spots ([QLD Fishing 2.0 on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=au.gov.qld.daff.qldfisheries&hl=en_AU)). Queensland's minister reported more than 80 downloads a day of the official app in its first five months with government promotion ([Queensland ministerial statement, Mar 2021](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/91625)).

**Alternatives and the reason to switch.** On Android AU, every state has a free official app and most are rated poorly with fresh 2026 complaints: FishSmart NSW 100K+ at 2.4 stars (broken edge-to-edge display July 2026, login loops, 2022 tide tables) ([FishSmart on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mobiddiction.fishsmart&hl=en_AU)); QLD Fishing 2.0 50K+ at 2.6 ("Every update wipes my stored data", 30 Jul 2026); Vic Fishing 10K+ at 3.3; SA Fishing 50K+ at 3.1 but relaunched with 5-star reviews from December 2025 ([SA Fishing on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=au.com.sra&hl=en_AU)); NT Fishing Mate 5K+ at 2.3, last updated November 2022; Recfishwest's WA app is the exception at 100K+ and 4.2 stars, free, ad-free and offline, run by a peak body rather than government ([Recfishwest on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.redi.rfw.production&hl=en_AU)). The earlier scan's premise that no third party covers all states offline is **wrong**: iDfish (Australian, since 2016; Play 50K+, 4.4 stars from 697 reviews, $9.90 a year, about 2 GB download, login required before offline use) advertises Australia-wide size limits and protection status offline; a January 2026 reviewer confirms "all the data is available on your phone so it's usable even when out of internet", while August 2026 reviewers report a stuck login screen and crashes ([iDfish on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=au.com.idfish.app&hl=en_AU); [iDfish on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/idfish/id1068033877), 4.66 stars from 1,457 ratings). FishOz, the closest analogue to the proposed product (modern rules engine, offline, WA only, launched October 2024), has 100+ Android installs and one iOS rating after two years and its developer is pivoting to a B2B rules-authoring tool ([FishOz on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.michaf.ozfish&hl=en_AU); [fish-oz.com](https://www.fish-oz.com/)). On iOS AU, the official apps are 2.34–3.3 stars (Vic Fishing "re-downloads each time it opens, so it's unusable without phone service", Jan 2023) ([Vic Fishing on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/vic-fishing/id493874267)). The US analogue, Fish Rules (500K+ Android installs, 3.2 stars, ads plus purchases up to US$42.99), is partly agency-funded and drew a November 2025 one-star review for placing "an ad between every fish name" ([Fish Rules on Play, US](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fishrulesapp.fishrules2013&hl=en&gl=US)). The credible switch reasons are no login, no forced update, instant search with aliases, one install for interstate trips, and free versus iDfish; they appeal to the avid minority, while the casual majority will keep googling "fish size limits nsw".

**Data, maintenance, demand and monetisation.** Rules are published as HTML tables, PDFs and legislation in seven formats; QLD and nsw.gov.au content is CC BY 4.0 at whole-of-government level ([qld.gov.au copyright](https://www.qld.gov.au/legal/copyright); [nsw.gov.au copyright](https://www.nsw.gov.au/copyright)), WA and Tasmania pages carry generic copyright footers ([DPIRD WA rules](https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/individuals/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-rules/); [Tasmanian limits table](https://fishing.tas.gov.au/recreational-fishing/rules/size-and-bag-limits)), and SA, VIC and NT pages were blocked. Roughly 20–25 discrete rule changes were dated in the twelve months to October 2026, and FishOz shipped five WA rules releases in ten months, so one to three data updates a month is the realistic floor. VRFish warns anglers not to rely on AI for rules and lists only official channels ([VRFish, Dec 2025](https://vrfish.com.au/2025/12/01/dont-rely-on-ai-for-fishing-rules/)); no state was found objecting to third-party apps. Australian search demand is state-fragmented: **"fish size limits" 50 a month, "nsw fish size limits" 590, "fish size limits qld" 320, "qld fish size limits app" 210, long tail about 2,680 a month** (Ubersuggest, data dated Sept–Oct 2026); "fishing rules app" 10. US and UK demand were not measured; the US is locked by Fish Rules. Every official app and Recfishwest is ad-free, the only paying Australian competitor charges $9.90 a year, and field sessions are short, bright-sun, wet-handed and 20–40% offline (estimate), so ads can only be an anchored banner plus perhaps an app-open ad on online launches; a one-off A$5–10 "support updates" purchase fits better. The ease score is 4 for the shell but 2 for data assembly and upkeep, blended **3; commercial 2; partially validated with the all-states premise corrected**.

### Logging sound meter with classroom mode: the differentiators already exist

**Target user and frequency.** Direct telemetry is not public; AppGoblin estimates show the leaders' monthly active users at 1–8% of lifetime installs (Splend ~26.1M installs, ~710K MAU; Smart Tools ~20.6M, ~1.1M; ABC ~24.4M, ~216K; KTW ~6.1M, ~509K), which means most installs are one-off curiosity sessions and installs, not retention, drive revenue ([AppGoblin, Splend](https://www.appgoblin.info/apps/com.splendapps.decibel); [AppGoblin, KTW](https://www.appgoblin.info/apps/com.ktwapps.soundmeter)). Repeat use comes from neighbour-noise documenters (bursts of one to three weeks, wanting hours of unattended logging) and workplace self-advocates; teachers overwhelmingly use free browser tools projected on a board (Classroomscreen's sound-level widget, Bouncy Balls) rather than phone apps ([WeAreTeachers, Jun 2024](https://www.weareteachers.com/classroom-noise-monitor-apps/); [Classroomscreen](https://classroomscreen.com/widgets/sound-level)). Autocomplete in both Australia and the US puts "for classroom" in the top five modifiers of "noise meter app", but the completions that follow ("monitor coral", "monitor ocean", "traffic light") are names of web tools ([Google Suggest, AU](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&gl=au&hl=en&q=sound%20meter%20app)). No keyword volumes were obtained for this finalist.

**Alternatives and the reason to switch.** Android is dominated by Splend's Sound Decibel Meter (10M+, 4.7 stars, 628K reviews; reviewers in June 2025, July 2026 and September 2026 describe "full-screen unskippable video ads... every 20 seconds" and readings that call a brew pub a "Quiet Library") ([Splend on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.splendapps.decibel&hl=en_AU)), Smart Tools (10M+, CSV export paid in a A$5.90 Pro app) ([Smart Tools on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=kr.sira.sound&hl=en_AU)), ABC Apps (10M+, banner-only, calibration per device; reviewers in September 2025 and September 2026 asked for exactly the timestamped logging and GPS tagging in this idea) ([ABC Apps on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.gamebasic.decibel&hl=en_AU)), KTW (5M+, "ads are unobtrusive at the bottom", Sept 2026) and Decibel X (5M+, subscription up to A$52.99, "80 bucks a year Canadian?" July 2026) ([Decibel X on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.skypaw.decibel&hl=en_AU)). The three proposed differentiators are already shipping: Calm dB (released 27 Dec 2025) advertises CSV/JSON export, calibration and no ads and has reached about 11K installs and 1.4K MAU ([Calm dB on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.github.indieappslab.calmdb&hl=en_AU); [AppGoblin](https://www.appgoblin.info/apps/io.github.indieappslab.calmdb)); Quiet Classroom (10K+ after six years) ranks first for classroom terms with a free alarm tier ([Quiet Classroom on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.quietclassroom.jd&hl=en_AU)); Noise Moderator is literally a traffic-light meter at 100K+ installs, abandoned in 2023 at 3.0 stars ([Noise Moderator on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sprintcoder.noisemoderator&hl=en_AU)); calibration offsets exist in ABC, KTW, Coocent and Decibel X. On iOS, Decibel X (US 4.65 stars from 163,282 ratings) removed all ads in 2021 in favour of a subscription (AU$79.99 a year), and the government-built NIOSH Sound Level Meter is free, ad-free and 4.67 stars ([Decibel X on the App Store, AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/decibel-x-db-noise-meter-slm/id448155923); [NIOSH SLM](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/niosh-sound-level-meter/id1096545820)). The earlier scan's "NIOSH broke for 16 kHz microphones" item is corrected: the error affected users routing audio through a Bluetooth hearing-aid microphone and was fixed in v1.5.0 (May 2026), not a hardware-generation break. The real gap is narrower than framed: a mainstream-quality, well-ranked app combining logging, calibration and banner-only ads, which is a UX and ranking problem, not a feature gap.

**Technical reality, policy, build and monetisation.** The measurement core is trivial, but phones under-read loud sounds and clip: in a 2025 NHS test on a Galaxy A54 at 85 dB, the top ten free Android apps read between 49 and 86 dB and none offered A-weighting ([Cureus, Mar 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12040400/)); NIOSH's 2016 study found no Android app met its criteria ([Kardous & Shaw 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5102154)); a KTW reviewer needed a +22 dB offset on a Poco F7 Pro. A classroom traffic light can stay foreground with the screen on and needs no foreground service, but hours-long neighbour logging needs a `microphone` foreground service, a Play Console declaration with a demo video, and handling of Android 10+ microphone arbitration ([Android foreground service types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types); [Play Console declaration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821)). AdMob forbids ads in the background and treats banners next to interactive elements or on continuously-interacted screens as a leading cause of invalid activity ([AdMob banner guidance](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6128877); [AdMob invalid traffic](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/3342054)), so the two longest-session modes (classroom display, overnight logging) should carry no ads at all, leaving revenue to the same short curiosity sessions the 10M+ incumbents monetise at 100–300 times the install velocity. The one post-2024 breakout (Beacon/Pony, ~273K installs in 12 months, now rated down for "constant full page ads") did it with a generic listing, not with features ([Pony on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pony.decibelmeter.soundmeter&hl=en_AU)). Market-clearing prices are about A$1–2 for remove-ads and A$5–6 for CSV; giving CSV away forgoes the one proven paid upsell. iOS is deferred indefinitely: it needs microphone and background-audio handling the developer cannot test, against a free government app. Validated scores: **ease 3, commercial 2; not validated as framed**.

### Offline expense splitter: a real use case that Splid already owns

**Target user and frequency.** The no-account pattern is real and freshly documented: Splid's twelve most recent AU App Store reviews (Aug–Oct 2026) are all travel-related, including "doesn't require everyone in the group to sign up" (24 Sep 2026) ([Splid AU reviews RSS](https://itunes.apple.com/au/rss/customerreviews/id=991473495/sortBy=mostRecent/json)); a 2024 Play reviewer praised entering "all expenses on one device (unlike some other apps that require all users to have the app)" ([Splid on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=splid.teamturtle.com.splid&hl=en_US&gl=US)). The open-source no-account web app Spliit reported ~300 new groups and ~2,000 expenses a week, with about half of real groups having more than five expenses ([Spliit by the stats, Oct 2024](https://spliit.app/blog/spliit-by-the-stats-usage-costs-donations)). Use is bursty: a trip organiser adds two to five expenses a day for one to two weeks, each a 20–60 second session, then goes dormant; couples and flat-shares add a handful a week. Nothing measures sessions for any splitter app.

**Alternatives and the reason to switch.** On Android, Splid (1M+, 87,419 ratings at 4.88, no ads, one-off "Splid Plus" A$5.99) advertises offline groups, no sign-up and optional sync, and users confirm it; its one visible vulnerability is no Android update since 23 August 2025. Tricount (10M+, 4.74, ads) and Settle Up (1M+, 4.73, ads plus IAP) are free; Splittr (100K+, 4.04, ads with US$14.99 lifetime removal) is the proposed model already in market ([Splittr on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.splittr.android&hl=en_US&gl=US)); WeXpense, Splital and Splitkaro sit at 100K+ with 4.45–4.88 stars; and 2024–2026 "offline/no account" entrants (Travel Split 5+ installs, Splitie 100+, Splitright 0 ratings after two years) show that shipping one produces no installs by itself ([Travel Split on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.travel.split.expense.tracker&hl=en_US&gl=US)). On iOS, Splid is 4.91 stars from 4,167 US ratings and 4.9 from 1,073 in AU, Tricount 4.88, and Spliit shipped a free, ad-free native iOS app with iCloud groups in September 2026 ([Spliit for iPhone](https://spliit.app/blog/spliit-for-iphone-has-been-rewritten-from-scratch)). Splitwise's degradation is the refugee source: Trustpilot AU scores it 2.0 from 72 reviews with "Can't add more than 2 expenses a day" (27 Aug 2026) ([Trustpilot AU](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/splitwise.com)), but refugees already land on Tricount, Settle Up and Splid, and Splitwise's Play listing carries no "Contains ads" flag and no detected ad SDK, so its "ads" are house promotions and should not be cited as evidence that SDK ads are tolerated here ([AppGoblin, Splitwise](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.Splitwise.SplitwiseMobile)). The specific wedge "no sync at all" is a strict subset of Splid's optional sync, and the only dated review about a sync-less app asks for sync to be added (WeXpense, 12 Feb 2026) ([WeXpense on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.expensecount.groupExpense&hl=en_US&gl=US)). The earlier scan's "tricount sync failures after the bunq takeover" is partly confirmed (a June 2025 data-loss review; the January 2026 complaint concerns bunq transfer fees, not sync).

**Demand, acquisition and monetisation.** US search demand is small and already farmed: **"bill splitting app" 3,600 a month, "split expenses app" 720, "splitwise alternative" 720 (SEO difficulty 29)**, and the entire "no account / offline / without login" modifier family has no reportable volume; "bill splitting app australia" is 10 a month (Ubersuggest, locId 2840, data dated Aug–Oct 2026). Australian and UK volumes were not pulled. Settle Up's founder names ASO, marketing, localisation and conversion as the hard problems after ten years with a five-person team ([FEE CTU interview, Nov 2025](https://fel.cvut.cz/en/what-s-on/news/82222-don-t-be-afraid-to-start-and-put-even-a-small-project-into-production-says-david-vavra-an-fee-ctu-graduate-and-author-of-the-popular-app-settle-up)); indie splitter launches on Hacker News in 2024–2026 scored one to five points. The defensible MVP features users actually ask for are transparent per-person calculation, an edit history, export that stays free and no daily caps; none needs a backend. Users volunteer willingness to pay a one-off few dollars ("Thank you for being a non greedy splitwise", Feb 2025) and complain about interstitials between entries (Settle Up, 9 Jul 2026) ([Settle Up on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cz.destil.settleup&hl=en_US&gl=US)). A hypothesised geography for an English-first Play launch is 30–40% US, 10–15% UK, 5–10% Australia, 25–35% India and other markets, which drags the blended eCPM toward the global average. Validated scores: **ease 4.5, commercial 1.5; not validated as a differentiated opportunity**.

## An ad model that needs thousands of users for the first AUD 100

The model counts only foreground impressions: no background, lock-screen or widget time, and no ads on launch, exit, mid-task or more than one full-screen ad per two user actions, which is AdMob's own rule ([AdMob disallowed interstitials](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6201362); [app-open guidance](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9341964?hl=en)). eCPM is revenue per thousand impressions shown; revenue per request also depends on match rate and show rate ([AdMob glossary](https://support.google.com/admob/table/9462111?hl=en)), for which no public benchmark by country exists, so the model applies an explicit haircut. The eCPM inputs are chart readings (±5–10%) from the only recent dataset with country-by-format values, Appodeal's report on Oct–Dec 2024 data from 100,000+ apps, which is framed around mobile games and shows December as the seasonal peak ([Appodeal eCPM report](https://appodeal.com/the-mobile-ecpm-report-updated-q4-2024-view)). A secondary quote of the same dataset gives figures 20–40% higher ([maf.ad](https://maf.ad/en/blog/mobile-ads-ecpm/)) and Tenjin/CAS.AI Q2 2024 game figures run 1.5–2× higher (Australia iOS rewarded US$19.91, interstitial US$16.54) ([GameDev Reports on Tenjin](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/tenjin-ad-monetization-in-mobile-00b?open=false)), so the Appodeal readings are the conservative end of a range. Nothing utility-specific exists, and utility sessions are low-intent, so these should be read as ceilings for the apps in this report.

**Generic scenarios for a single-purpose utility.** Each scenario fixes geography, impressions per active day, match-and-show rate and a seasonal factor (Q4 is the peak; one developer's dataset showed a ~35% drop from December 2025 to January 2026 ([playio summary](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-ecpm-benchmarks-2026))). Revenue per daily active user per day equals the sum over formats of slots × eCPM/1,000 × match-and-show × seasonal factor. AUD 100 a month is US$70.10, or US$2.337 a day over 30 days.

| Interstitial eCPM (US$) | 1.65 | 6.3 | 11.0 |

| Foreground minutes per session | 1.5 | 1.5 | 2 |

**Revenue is not profit.** AdMob's eCPM is already net of Google's share, so the ad line carries no further platform fee, but a "remove ads" purchase does: Google Play's new fee structure takes effect in Australia on 30 September 2026 at 10% plus a 5% billing fee on the first US$1M, so about 15% of a Play-billed purchase ([Play service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16954621)); Apple's Small Business Program is 15% under US$1M ([Apple SBP](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)). An Australian developer must charge and remit 10% GST on Play purchases by Australian customers and needs an ABN; Apple acts as agent and requires Australian developers to hold an ABN and be GST-registered before selling paid apps ([Play tax help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000?hl=en-GB); [42 Advisory, Jun 2026](https://42advisory.com.au/42-advisory-blog/gst-app-store-sales-australia)). AdMob income is invoiced by Google Asia Pacific in Singapore; the common practitioner position is that it is a GST-free export of services, but no ATO source confirming this for AdMob was found and the ATO pages returned 403, so obtain advice; it is assessable for income tax regardless. Payment arrives around the 21st of the month only once the balance reaches **AUD 100**, otherwise it rolls over ([AdMob payment thresholds](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/2772208?hl=en-AU); [payment timeline](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/2772140?hl=en-AU)), so an app earning AUD 30 a month is paid quarterly and a new account earns nothing for its first several months.

On the penalty checker's base economics (US$0.027 per MAU-month), Apple's membership alone requires about **300 sustained iOS monthly active users every month just to break even**, before any iOS testing cost. Against that, the only population-level distribution found for small apps (subscription apps on RevenueCat, 115,000+ apps) puts median revenue one year after launch at about **US$72 a month**, with 17.3% of new apps reaching US$1K a month within two years ([RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026, Utilities](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-utilities/)); it is a different monetisation model and a self-selected sample, but it is the only honest anchor for "typical", and no representative dataset of AdMob earnings exists. Claims about typical developer earnings, time to profitability or how many apps one must publish are not supported by anything found and are not made here.

## Cheap demand tests with hard pass and fail lines

Each test is designed to cost little more than the Play fee and a domain, to run for 60 to 90 days, and to measure acquisition and return use rather than whether the use case exists. Thresholds are the researchers' judgements anchored to the cited comparators, not measured benchmarks.

**Penalty-rate checker.** Publish the real two-award (General Retail and Hospitality) calculator as a static web page with analytics, FWC CC-BY attribution, a disclaimer and an Android waitlist button; add one SEO article per state and a few short videos timed to the 1 December 2026 junior-rate change. **Pass by week 12:** at least 1,000 organic sessions a month, 40% or more of sessions completing a calculation, a 30-day return rate of 15% or more, and 150 waitlist sign-ups or a 5% click-through to Play. Only then publish a Play open-test listing; **pass** is 500 organic installs in eight weeks with D30 retention of 15% and a median of two sessions a week among retained users, which is the log-plus-check behaviour the ad model requires. **Fail:** page ranks on page one yet draws under 300 sessions a month, completion under 25%, 30-day return under 5%, or under 50 sign-ups. **Kill signals:** PayJar still at single-digit ratings in January 2027 despite being the only iOS equivalent; the FWO adds pay calculation to Record My Hours or PACT ships a mobile shift log.

**Caravan weight calculator.** Publish a one-page web calculator with the glossary and an "offline Android app" button, ship the same calculator as an Android open-test build, post once in each of the two largest caravanning Facebook groups and hand a QR flyer at one council weigh day, then run 60 days with event logging only. **Pass (all three):** at least 500 web sessions in days 31–60 with 25% from organic search; at least 300 installs with 15% of installers opening the app on a second calendar day after day seven and 10% completing a second full calculation; at least 10 unprompted ratings averaging 4.2 or 20 saved rigs per 100 installs. **Fail:** under 150 installs, a D30 open rate under 8%, or a median of one calculation per user. **Kill signals:** the page cannot reach page one for a difficulty-8 term within 90 days; comments repeat "used it once at purchase"; loadmate or Weigh That Tow ships a free unlimited tier.

**Fish size and bag limits.** Build a two-state (NSW and QLD, both CC BY 4.0) Android-only offline MVP with per-state SEO pages ("NSW fish size limits 2026" tables) carrying a "download the offline app" button and a "notify me for your state" field; post once in two fishing groups per state and one forum thread, then stop promoting and run 60 days. **Pass (all three):** at least 500 organic installs (five times FishOz's two-year total, one-tenth of the QLD government's promoted rate); D30 retention of 10% with a median of two sessions per retained user per month; at least 100 "notify me" sign-ups for other states. **Fail:** under 150 installs, D30 under 5%, median session under 45 seconds with under one session a month per retained user, or any state limit change missed for more than 14 days (upkeep is unsustainable). **Kill signals:** SA or QLD adds true offline mode and drops login; iDfish adds a free tier; Fishbrain or Fishing Points adds Australian regulations.

**Logging sound meter.** Test A (one week, no cost): install Calm dB, Quiet Classroom, KTW and Smart Tools on the developer's phone and confirm whether any 10K+-install app rated 4.3 or higher already offers free timestamped CSV, calibration and no interstitials with a 2026 update; if so, the logging differentiator is dead. Test B (two weeks): a minimal Play listing or pre-registration page titled around "free CSV log and classroom light", en_AU and en_US, no paid traffic; **pass** is 300 organic installs or pre-registrations in 14 days with 30% opening the classroom screen and 10% exporting a CSV; **fail** is under 100 installs or under 5% CSV use. Test C (parallel): the classroom traffic light as a free web page shared in two teacher communities; **pass** is 50 sessions a week sustained for three weeks with any unsolicited request for a phone version. **Kill:** Test A finds the incumbent, Test B yields under 100 installs, or curiosity sessions average under two minutes while the classroom screen must stay ad-free.

**Offline expense splitter.** Step 1 (one to two weeks): a static "who owes whom" calculator page targeting "expense splitter calculator / online free / excel template" terms with a Play pre-registration button; **pass** is 300 organic visitors a month by month three with an 8% store click-through; **fail** is under 100 visitors a month with ten indexed pages. Step 2 (four to six weeks of build): a Play-only MVP with offline groups, weighted splits, minimal transfers, image share, an anchored banner, one interstitial at settle-up and a US$2.99–3.99 remove-ads purchase; **pass over 60 days** is 500 organic installs, 40% of installers creating a group with five or more expenses, 30% of real groups sharing, D30 organiser retention of 10%, remove-ads conversion of 1% of real-group creators and a 4.5 rating from 30 ratings; **fail** is under 150 installs or 20% of reviews citing missing sync as a one-to-three-star reason. The disconfirming evidence is already largely on record (Splid, zero-volume keywords, 0–5-rating entrants, users requesting sync), so this test should run only if the developer has a distribution wedge such as a travel-blog placement in hand.

## What is still unknown, and what would reorder the list

The biggest uncertainty is engagement: no source measures sessions per month, session length or retention for any of the five finalists or their competitors, so every usage input in the model is inferred from review text and app purpose. The second is yield: the only country-by-format eCPM tables are for games in Q4 2024, no utility-specific or Australian fill-rate benchmark exists, Tenjin's figures are 1.5–2× Appodeal's for the same countries, and secondary quotes of Appodeal differ by 20–40%, so the base-case MAU counts above could be off by a factor of two in either direction. The third is demand measurement, where the environment itself removed several signals: Ubersuggest was rationed to a single keyword-suggestions report per finalist (Australian reports for the penalty-rate, caravan and fish ideas, one US report for the splitter, none for the sound meter), so no AU volumes exist for the splitter, no US or UK volumes for the three Australian ideas, and no monthly trend series for anything, and its volumes are modelled (±50%); Play Store keyword volumes are unobservable (Play's autocomplete endpoint returns 404); Google Trends returned HTTP 429 throughout, so seasonality could not be quantified for any finalist; reddit.com was blocked and no Reddit thread surfaced in any search snippet, so the user-testimony channel most developers rely on is absent; forums.whirlpool.net.au returned a 403 challenge; AppBrain's app pages were blocked (only its category headline figure was read); Facebook group sizes are 2018 counts; and AppGoblin's install, MAU and revenue figures use an undisclosed methodology and are directional only. Absence from a Play or App Store search result was never treated as absence of a competitor: the "PayJar not on Android" and "ShiftTrak not found" observations are labelled unverified for that reason. Primary sources that could not be opened and are cited via snippets or secondary pages include the FWC wage-review PDFs and MAPD API documentation (403), fairwork.gov.au (403/503), the FRDC survey PDFs and the NSW, QLD, SA, VIC and NT fisheries rule pages (Cloudflare challenge), OAIC and ATO pages (404/403), Business of Apps (403), the JetBrains Compose Multiplatform post (403), MacinCloud pricing (404), and the NYT vibe-coding article (paywall). Apple's customer-review RSS feeds returned empty for most apps tested, so iOS review text is limited to the four reviews shown on each listing page. On the regulatory side it is unresolved whether a sub-A$3m solo developer sharing device identifiers with an ad SDK falls inside the Privacy Act small-business exemption or its "trading in personal information" carve-out, whether AdMob monetisation (as opposed to Play billing) triggers Google's display of the developer's full address, and how the ATO treats AdMob income for GST.

Several specific findings would change the ranking. The penalty-rate checker moves up if PayJar or a similar unmarketed app shows real traction by early 2027 (proving the segment installs such apps) or if the staged web test shows return rates above 15%; it moves down if the FWO adds pay calculation to Record My Hours. The caravan and fish ideas move up only if a free, maintained web page proves it can rank and convert at several hundred installs a month, or if a state fisheries agency abandons its app; they are dead the moment loadmate, Weigh That Tow or iDfish offers a free unlimited tier, or an official app ships true offline mode. The sound meter moves up only if Test A finds that no 10K+ app actually offers free CSV without interstitials and Test B shows classroom intent converting on phones; it moves down if Calm dB's export is confirmed free. The splitter moves up only if Splid's Android update gap (since 23 Aug 2025) turns into visible decay in its rating stream, which it has not yet. Outside the five, two ideas gained rather than lost evidence during the scans and deserve the same cheap test before anything else is built: a permission-free bubble level timed against Lemondo's September 2026 session paywall on iOS and Gamma Play's stale Android leader, and the AS/NZS 3008 cable-size calculator sitting under a 100K+ app rated 1.0 from 813 reviews and abandoned since 2023 (subject to a copyright check on reproducing standards-derived tables). Given that every finalist dropped one to two points on validation, those scan scores of 3 and 3.5 should be expected to fall as well.

## Platform and tooling: ship Android first, treat iOS as a cost to be earned

For this developer the stack decision is mostly settled by the evidence. The lowest-risk Android-only path is native Kotlin with Jetpack Compose and Google's first-party AdMob SDK, with no cross-platform layer; it is the stack of the documented 2026 non-programmer success ([HN, GreenDot](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48344627)), and a pure Kotlin app with no native libraries already satisfies Play's 16 KB page-size rule, whereas Flutter, React Native and the AdMob SDK all ship native code that must be rebuilt by 1 February 2027 ([Android 16 KB page sizes](https://developer.android.com/guide/practices/page-sizes)). If iOS is wanted later, the two stacks with mature, documented AdMob integration and a no-Mac path are Flutter (Google's `google_mobile_ads` plugin, version 9.2.0 published on 22 Oct 2026, with an official cookbook) and Expo/React Native (Invertase's library, seven releases between 18 Sept and 21 Oct 2026) ([Flutter cookbook](https://docs.flutter.dev/cookbook/plugins/google-mobile-ads); [pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/google_mobile_ads); [react-native-google-mobile-ads releases](https://github.com/invertase/react-native-google-mobile-ads/releases)). Both carry churn a hobby app will feel: Flutter documented 49 breaking changes across seven stable releases from 3.29 to 3.47 ([Flutter breaking changes](https://docs.flutter.dev/release/breaking-changes)), and the RN AdMob library's v17 (Sept 2026) dropped the legacy architecture entirely. Compose Multiplatform iOS is stable but depends on community AdMob wrappers with tens of GitHub stars; Capacitor's AdMob plugin is community-maintained with a paid alternative; MAUI's popular plugin gates EU consent behind a licence. An app left untouched for a year will need a framework upgrade and an ad-SDK major bump before its next forced Play release.

Google Play's requirements for a new personal account are concrete and dated. The fee is US$25 once; the developer must be 18 or over, verify identity and verify an Android device through the Play Console app; and accounts created after 13 November 2023 must run a closed test with **at least 12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days** before applying for production, with review usually within seven days ([Play closed testing](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en-GB)); the earlier "20 testers" figure is not on Google's current page and a tester-for-hire market exists. Google displays a personal account's legal name, country and developer email, and its full address "if you decide to monetize on Google Play" ([Play public developer info](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312)); whether AdMob counts is not stated, so assume a residential address could become public unless an alternative address is used. Recurring deadlines set the maintenance floor: new apps and updates must target API 36 by 31 August 2026 (extension to 1 November 2026) and the bar rises every year ([target API requirements](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk)); 16 KB support is mandatory for updates from 1 February 2027; app registration under Android developer verification goes global in 2027 ([developer verification](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification)); and policy announcements landed on at least two dates in 2026 ([Play policy announcements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9934569)). Every app needs a privacy policy link in the console and in-app even if it collects nothing ([Play User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)); AdMob additionally requires an app-ads.txt file on the developer's website for apps added since January 2025 (secondary report) ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/new-admob-policy-requires-app-ads-txt-from-january-2025/)) and a certified consent platform for EEA/UK/Swiss users ([AdMob CMP requirement](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/13554116)). AdMob reviews new accounts within about 24 hours (rarely two weeks) and new apps in two to three days with limited serving meanwhile ([AdMob account approval](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9905175); [AdMob app review](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/10564477?hl=en)), and testing with live ad units on one's own phone is the classic route to an invalid-traffic suspension of "most frequently 30 days" (search-snippet only) ([AdMob blog](https://admob.googleblog.com/search/label/App%20Best%20Practices)), so use Google's test IDs and register the phone as a test device. One phone plus Android Studio emulators (including a 16 KB image) is sufficient for a permission-free utility; OEM coverage can be sampled on Firebase Test Lab's free tier (five physical runs a day, shutting down 30 September 2027) ([Firebase Test Lab pricing](https://firebase.google.com/docs/test-lab/usage-quotas-pricing)).

iOS without a Mac is a documented, supported workflow, not a workaround. EAS Build runs on Mac mini hosts, EAS Submit "works on macOS, Linux, and Windows", and Codemagic can generate Apple certificates from an App Store Connect API key ([EAS Submit docs](https://docs.expo.dev/submit/ios/); [Codemagic iOS signing](https://docs.codemagic.io/yaml-code-signing/signing-ios/)); Apple's own forums recommend GitHub Actions macOS runners for exactly this ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/815497)). Hobby volume fits in free tiers (15 EAS iOS builds; 500 Codemagic minutes; roughly eight 23-minute builds on GitHub Free), and the expensive route, renting a Mac, starts at about AUD 155 a month on MacStadium or a 24-hour minimum of about AUD 4 on Scaleway ([MacStadium pricing](https://www.macstadium.com/pricing); [Scaleway Apple silicon](https://www.scaleway.com/en/pricing/apple-silicon/)). The weak link is testing: cloud simulators cannot test the camera, push, biometrics or real performance, Expo's EAS Simulator is waitlist-only as of September–October 2026, Appetize offers 30 free minutes a month, and Expo itself recommends a borrowed iPhone via TestFlight, whose builds expire after 90 days ([Expo, test iOS without a Mac, 7 Oct 2026](https://expo.dev/articles/testing-and-previewing/test-ios-app-without-mac)). Apple-side steps dominate time-to-first-build: membership processing, 24–72 hours to register a device, and Beta App Review for external testers. The Apple Developer Program costs US$99 a year "in local currency where available" (AUD 141 at the RBA rate; AUD 149 observed in 2025), the enrolling individual's legal name becomes the public seller, and the card must be in that name ([Apple enrollment support](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/)).

The review risk on iOS is codified and current. Guideline 4.2 rejects apps that are not "particularly useful, unique, or 'app-like'", 4.2.6 rejects apps "created from a commercialized template or app generation service", and 4.3(b) refuses new flashlight, sound-effect, wallpaper and simple-timer apps outright unless "meaningfully different" ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Apple rejected 2,093,244 of 9,100,620 submissions in 2025, 415,532 of them under the Design section that includes minimum functionality and spam, while 387,087 rejections were later approved after changes ([Apple App Store Transparency Report 2025](https://www.apple.com/legal/app-store/transparency/2025)); in March 2026 a deliberately minimal but functional TV tracker was rejected because "the usefulness of the app is limited by the minimal functionality it currently provides" ([Apple Developer Forums, Mar 2026](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/817622)). All five finalists are single-purpose utilities, so an iOS submission should expect to argue offline depth, saved state and widgets in its review notes. For the three Australia-only finalists, iOS would roughly double the reachable audience (iDfish has 1,457 iOS ratings against 697 Play reviews, suggesting iOS is the larger paying segment for fishing apps), yet the developer cannot test microphone or sensor behaviour on a device, and on the base economics each needs several hundred sustained iOS users just to pay Apple's fee. The defensible sequence is therefore Android-only, Kotlin/Compose or Flutter, with the privacy policy and app-ads.txt on a free static host, and iOS added only to an app that has already passed its demand test and is clearing the AUD 100 threshold monthly.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. The constraint on a solo Australian vibe-coder is not building, which all five finalists prove is a weekend-to-few-weeks job, and not even distribution in the abstract, but the arithmetic of attention: a useful single-purpose utility is used briefly and rarely, AdMob's own rules cap how many ads can be shown in that time, and a tier-1 Australian audience of a few thousand people is still worth only a few cents each per month. The finalists that look most Australian and least contested (caravan weights, fish limits) are exactly the ones with the least foreground time and the lowest search volumes, while the ideas with real foreground time (sound meter, splitter) face 10M+ incumbents and global audiences priced at the blended eCPM. "None sufficiently validated yet" is the supported conclusion, and the monetisation shape the evidence favours is the inverse of the brief's premise: ads as the free-tier acquisition wedge, a one-off remove-ads purchase at the "flat white" price point as the actual revenue, and a web companion page whose per-click value in these niches exceeds anything a banner earns in-app.

If the developer proceeds anyway, the order is set by the tests, not the scores: run the penalty-rate checker's zero-build web test first, because it is the only idea where a tier-1 audience, an uncontested keyword and free official data coincide; keep the caravan calculator as a cheap portfolio companion only if the web page ranks; and drop the fish, sound-meter and splitter ideas unless their specific kill conditions turn out not to hold. The ranking will move as the blocked signals come back: a single afternoon of Google Trends, Reddit and Play keyword data, when available, would resolve more uncertainty than any further desk research in this environment.
